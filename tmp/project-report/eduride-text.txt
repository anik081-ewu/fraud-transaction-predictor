Abstract
The EduRide Transport Management System is a comprehensive software solution
designed to streamline campus transportation operations. By replacing manual
processes with an automated, user-centric platform, the system aims to reduce
administrative burdens, enhance resource optimization, and improve service quality
for all stakeholders, including students, teachers, administrators, staff, and drivers.
Built using the MERN stack (MongoDB, Express.js, React.js, Node.js), the
application features role-specific dashboards that facilitate route management, real-
time tracking, payments, and communication. This report outlines the project's
motivation, description, technical strategy, and implementation details, highlighting
its potential to deliver efficiency, reliability, and user empowerment in educational
environments.
Keywords: EduRide Transport Management System, Campus Transportation,
Transport Management System, Real-time Vehicle Tracking, MERN Stack,
MongoDB, Express.js, React.js, Node.js.

Chapter 1
Introduction
1.1 Background
Transportation management is a crucial operational component of educational institutions
such as universities and colleges. Every day, students, teachers, staff, and drivers depend
on campus transportation services to ensure smooth commuting between residential areas
and academic premises. However, many institutions still rely on manual systems for
managing transport services, which creates inefficiency, confusion, scheduling conflicts,
lack of transparency, and increased administrative workload. With the advancement of
modern web technologies, it has become possible to develop intelligent transport
management platforms that provide real-time tracking, automated scheduling, digital
enrollment, and seamless communication among all stakeholders. The EduRide Transport
Management System has been developed to address these challenges by offering a smart,
reliable, and scalable campus transport solution.
1.2 Motivation
The motivation behind EduRide arises from the difficulties faced in traditional campus
transport systems. Manual operations require excessive paperwork, physical approvals,
and constant communication between transport offices and users.
EduRide enhances the campus transport experience by:
Reducing administrative hassle.

Digitizing route and slot allocation.

Providing real-time vehicle tracking.

Ensuring transparency in enrollment and payments.




1.3 Problem Statement
Manual transport management systems in educational institutions suffer from several
limitations:
Difficulty in managing multiple routes and time slots.

Lack of live vehicle monitoring.

Inefficient driver and vehicle assignment.

system.
1.4 Project Objectives
The EduRide Transport Management System aims to improve the efficiency of campus
transportation by introducing a centralized and automated management platform. The
system focuses on reducing manual administrative work and providing a structured
digital solution for managing transport operations. It provides role-based dashboards for
different users such as administrators, drivers, and passengers to ensure organized access
and management of transport services. The system also supports dynamic route and slot
selection, allowing users to enroll in available transport routes conveniently.

Literature Review
2.1 Overview
Transport management systems have been widely adopted in modern smart campuses.
Many institutions use digital systems for tracking buses, managing routes, and providing
transport-related services.
A transport management system typically includes:
Vehicle scheduling.

Route optimization.

Driver assignment.

GPS-based tracking.

User enrollment.

Payment processing.

Complaint and feedback support.

EduRide follows these principles while focusing on university campus needs.
2.2 Research Gap
Despite the increasing adoption of digital technologies in transportation systems, many
institutional transport management frameworks remain limited in scope and functionality.
Existing approaches primarily focus on isolated operational tasks such as payment
processing, route listing, or driver assignment, rather than offering a fully integrated
transport management ecosystem. In traditional campus environments, transport
administration is still dependent on manual procedures including paper-based payment
Page 11 of 47

=== PAGE 12 ===
verification, manual route allocation, and informal complaint handling mechanisms [1],
[2]. These processes introduce inefficiencies, increase administrative workload, and
create a high probability of human error.
Although some modern systems have attempted to digitize transport management through
web-based platforms, they often fail to address the comprehensive needs of a university
transport environment. Most existing solutions lack real-time vehicle monitoring
capabilities, which are critical for improving operational transparency, passenger safety,
and service reliability [1], [2]. Additionally, many systems do not implement role-based
access control structures, resulting in limited usability for different stakeholders such as
administrators, drivers, and passengers who require distinct operational interfaces and
permissions [3], [4].
Another significant limitation observed in current literature and practical
implementations is the absence of structured route-slot enrollment mechanisms tailored
for campus transportation. Many transport platforms operate with static route
assignments rather than dynamic slot-based enrollment systems that allow users to
register for specific routes and schedules efficiently. Furthermore, existing systems rarely
incorporate integrated communication modules for delivering transport notices,
operational updates, or traffic law awareness to users within the same platform.
From a research perspective, the major gap lies in the lack of a comprehensive,
university-focused transport management framework that integrates administrative
control, passenger services, operational monitoring, and communication functionalities
into a unified digital system. Current solutions are fragmented, focusing on single
functionalities rather than providing a holistic management environment capable of
supporting large academic institutions.

The EduRide Transport Management System must satisfy several important system
requirements to ensure effective and reliable operation. The system should be scalable,
allowing it to handle an increasing number of users, vehicles, and transport routes as the
university grows. It must also maintain a high level of reliability, ensuring that the system
remains stable and operational without frequent failures. Security is another critical

guaranteeing that all transport-related data such as routes, payments, and user information

The EduRide Transport Management System is a centralized, role-based platform
developed to efficiently manage school or college transportation services. The system
begins with a secure login management process where students, teachers, staff, drivers,
and administrators authenticate themselves using valid credentials. These credentials are
verified against a central database to ensure authorized access and maintain system
security. After successful authentication, users are directed to their respective dashboards
based on their assigned roles and permissions.
The core system functions as a central coordinator that integrates multiple management
modules, including user management, admin management, vehicle and bus request
management, and payment management. Users can request vehicles or bus services
through the system, and administrators can manage and assign transport resources
accordingly. The payment management module handles transport fees and securely
communicates with an external payment gateway to process transactions. The gateway
returns real-time payment status updates to ensure transparency and accuracy. Overall,
the system ensures secure, efficient, and accountable transport operations through
integrated authentication, centralized database control, vehicle allocation, and online
payment processing.

4.2.2 DFD Level 1 ( Admin )
Figure 4: DFD Level 1 ( Admin ).
Page 24 of 47

=== PAGE 25 ===
The diagram ( Figure 4 ) represents the Admin side of the transport management system.
First, the Admin logs in through the 1.0 Authentication module. The login information is
verified using the User DB, and after successful authentication, the admin can access all
management modules. The admin can manage complaints in the 2.0 Complaint module
by viewing student complaints and giving feedback. All complaint data is stored in the
Complaints DB. Through the 3.0 Assignment module, the admin manages bus
assignments, which are stored in the Bus DB. In the 4.0 Payment Overview and 12.0
Verify Student Payment modules, the admin views payment records and verifies paid
students using the Payment DB. The 5.0 Route & Stop module allows the admin to add,
edit, and manage routes and stops, with data stored in the Route DB. The 6.0 Fleet
module is used to add, edit, or delete vehicles, and the data is stored in the Vehicle DB.
The admin can publish announcements through the 10.0 Notice module (stored in the
Notice DB) and upload traffic rules through the 11.0 Traffic Rules module (stored in the
Traffic Law DB). Using the 8.0 Manage User module, the admin can add, update, delete,
or redefine user roles. The 9.0 Bus Request module allows the admin to review, approve,
or reject bus requests from students. The 7.0 Live Map module enables the admin to track
vehicles in real time using GPS.
Overall, the admin system controls and manages users, vehicles, routes, payments,
complaints, notices, and real-time tracking to ensure smooth transport service operation.

7.1 Overview
The EduRide Transport Management System has been successfully developed to address
the challenges associated with manual campus transport operations. This project aimed to
automate transport tasks such as enrollment, route and slot allocation, real-time vehicle
tracking, payment handling, complaints management, and communication through an
integrated digital platform. Built using modern web technologies, the system
demonstrates robust functionality, scalability, and improved user experience for all
stakeholders including administrators, students, teachers, staff, and drivers.
Comprehensive testing—including unit, integration, and performance evaluations—was
conducted to ensure that the system meets both functional and non-functional
requirements. The system’s ability to handle multiple users, real-time GPS updates, and
secure transactions has been verified under moderate load conditions, confirming its
readiness for practical deployment in an educational environment.
7.2 Limitation of the Project
While EduRide significantly improves the transport management process, several
limitations were identified during development and testing:
Dependency on Internet Connectivity:
Real-time GPS tracking and online payment features require stable internet access. In
areas with poor network coverage, system responsiveness may degrade.

Driver Phone-Based Tracking:
Currently, location updates rely on the driver’s mobile device. This approach may be

Scalability Constraint in High Load Scenarios:

User Role Dependencies:
